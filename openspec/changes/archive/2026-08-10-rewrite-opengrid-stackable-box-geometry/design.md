## Context

The current implementation starts with a full rounded solid, fuses a rounded rail ring, cuts the upper profile once per side, cuts the lower perimeter once per side, fuses seam cutters, and then cuts holes in batches. The reference STEP describes a single continuous perimeter profile: the upper rail changes its inner boundary through the 1.75 / 1.2 / 0.8 / 1.8 / 2.0 sequence while the outer boundary returns to the rounded side wall. The existing side-by-side cutters do not carry that profile continuously through the rounded corners.

The rewrite must keep the existing 28 mm footprint, fixed 5.0 mm bottom assembly, clear-interior-height semantics, hole contracts, Snap validation, and export interfaces.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Generate the outer bottom guide and upper rail from continuous rounded-rectangle sections.
- Preserve the reference 45° profile transitions and rounded corner continuity.
- Reduce the number of boolean operations on the normal build path.
- Keep the generated result a single valid solid with a continuous interior floor.
- Make the geometry evidence and tests describe the new construction rather than the discarded ring-and-cutter path.

**Non-Goals:**

- Adding wall, rail, guide, clearance, or performance controls to the user-facing parameter payload.
- Copying the reference model's overall footprint or hole coordinates.
- Changing the separate OpenGrid generator or OpenGrid Snap reference model.
- Preserving mating compatibility with solids generated by the immediately preceding profile.

## Decisions

### 1. Use a ruled loft for the full outer envelope

Construct the solid outer envelope from rounded-rectangle sections at the fixed lower profile datums and the top datum:

```text
z = 0       footprint inset by 2.95 mm, corner radius 3.75 - 2.95
z = 0.8     footprint inset by 2.15 mm, corner radius 3.75 - 2.15
z = 2.6     same supported vertical band
z = 4.75    full footprint,               corner radius 3.75
z = 5.0     full footprint,               corner radius 3.75
z = top     full footprint,               corner radius 3.75
```

The two lower ruled transitions are the bed-facing 0.8 mm chamfer and the reference-aligned 2.15 mm outer transition into the floor envelope. The fixed 5.0 mm bottom datum is preserved. Internal cell-seam reliefs use the 3.8 mm datum as the start of their final 45° closure and continue to a 4.6 mm central apex, so the seam does not create a horizontal upper land. The apex remains below the outer floor-envelope transition and the cavity opening, so the relief does not cut into the box interior. This produces the printable lower outline without cutting four side tools into a prebuilt prism.

The alternative of keeping the full prism and applying four perimeter cutters was rejected because it creates four extra boolean stages, makes corner results depend on cutter ordering, and was the source of clipped edge artifacts.

### 2. Use a second ruled loft for the interior and upper rail boundary

The cavity is a rounded-rectangle loft beginning at the upper surface of the fixed bottom assembly. Its section sequence follows the inner boundary of the reference rail:

```text
z = bottomAssemblyHeight       wall thickness / inner wall radius
z + 1.75                       inward 45° lead-in
z + 1.75 + 1.2                 vertical sliding-block face
z + 1.75 + 1.2 + 0.8           outward 45° transition
z + 1.75 + 1.2 + 0.8 + 1.8     vertical face
z + 7.55                       2.0 mm 45° return to the side wall
```

The section dimensions and corner radii are derived from the same outer rounded path and radial offsets. The lower cavity section uses the nominal 1.2 mm wall and 2.55 mm inner radius; the first rail section reaches the compact 0.8 mm inner corner radius. The final section leaves the fixed thin top return inside the outer envelope. `ruled` lofting keeps each intended transition planar and at 45°.

Cutting this single cavity from the outer loft leaves the upper rail fused to the wall by construction. It avoids the old approach of retaining a solid top cap and removing it with four independent profile tools.

The alternative of sweeping only a positive rail onto a conventional shell was rejected: the conventional shell's inner wall would remain vertical above the sliding block and would fill the reference return profile.

### 3. Batch independent relief and hole tools as compounds

Create one compound per seam axis for the 28 mm relief tools and apply at most two cuts to the outer/cavity result. Create one compound for the special Snap sockets and a second compound for ordinary bottom holes and apply one cut per class. The tools remain independent solids inside the compounds, so crossing seams and the two-stage socket shoulders retain their exact profiles without a serial cut for every cell.

If a kernel build rejects a compound containing intersecting seam tools, the fallback is two compounds (one per axis) and two cuts; the profile construction still remains loft-based and avoids the old four-side trim path.

### 4. Keep quality probes at observable interfaces

Retain the existing floor, wall, hole, Snap, stacking, sliding, bridge, and export checks. Add or update section probes that verify the upper rail's straight-side profile and its rounded-corner continuation. Quality checks must inspect the final solid rather than asserting implementation details such as a particular number of lofts.

### 5. Preserve cancellation and ownership boundaries

The builder continues checking the generation context between expensive stages. Temporary section wires and tool compounds are deleted only after their consuming loft or boolean operation completes; the final shape remains owned by the caller. This avoids stale generation commits and prevents the cleanup behavior of compound children from invalidating a still-used tool.

### 6. Rebuild-time evidence

On the local OpenCascade worker, a representative 1×1×10 mm shell build measured approximately 276 ms for the outer-envelope loft plus cavity loft cut, and the no-seam stacking stage measured below 1 ms. The complete 24-case B-Rep integration suite, including quality probes and STEP/STL export, completed in roughly 108–111 seconds on the same workspace. These numbers are recorded as evidence rather than a hard CI timeout because CAD kernel and worker startup performance varies by machine.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[Loft correspondence can change corner parameterization]** → Use matching rounded-rectangle wire constructors and ruled lofts, then assert straight-side profile spans and corner radius bounds in the quality gate.
- **[A compound cut may be less tolerant of crossing seam tools]** → Exercise crossed X/Y seams in 2×2 and larger fixtures; fall back to two axis compounds if the kernel reports an invalid result.
- **[The fixed 5 mm datum and reference outer transition have different horizontal/vertical runs]** → Keep the 5 mm interior-floor datum, use the reference 2.15 mm perimeter transition as the outer envelope, and extend the internal seam's 45° relief from the 3.8 mm datum to its 4.6 mm central apex without opening the cavity.
- **[The rewritten profile intentionally breaks old mating geometry]** → Document the break in the change and validate only against the current reference-aligned interface.
- **[Lofting large footprints can still be expensive]** → Keep the section count fixed and batch all independent relief and hole tools instead of scaling boolean stages with side count.

## Migration Plan

1. Add section/profile helpers and replace the shell, upper rail, and lower perimeter construction.
2. Batch seam, special-socket, and ordinary-hole tools while preserving their dimensions.
3. Update quality probes, failure mapping, and tests for the continuous corner profile.
4. Run targeted B-Rep, Worker, export, build, and OpenSpec validation.
5. If the result is rejected, revert the geometry module and related quality/test changes together; no persisted model data migration is required.
